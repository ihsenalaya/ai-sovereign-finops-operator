Article scope note
==================

The revised article is a bounded systems paper about governance-aware routing for
networked LLM applications.

Core problem
------------

An enterprise LLM application is a networked service: requests enter through a
gateway, are attributed to a Kubernetes workload, and are routed to a managed
model endpoint. The routing choice affects:

- cost;
- latency;
- declared data-residency policy;
- sensitive-data routing;
- namespace/team budgets;
- auditability.

Main contribution
-----------------

The artifact implements a Kubernetes-native control plane that:

- represents providers, models, budgets, routing policies, quality gates and
  reports as Kubernetes resources;
- filters candidates using declared provider metadata;
- ranks feasible candidates using cost, quality, latency, reliability and budget
  pressure;
- reports workload-level attribution;
- ingests Envoy AI Gateway telemetry;
- ingests shadow-AI evidence from gateway-independent egress observations.

Evidence boundary
-----------------

The paper separates evidence as:

- MEASURED: live calls, uncached repetitions, or integration logs;
- CACHED: replay/reanalysis of previously collected real API calls;
- SIMULATED: deterministic policy or budget scenarios over declared metadata and
  existing result rows.

The article must not overclaim. Declared-policy compliance is not legal
certification. Open-ended judge quality is not objective correctness. The
objective GSM8K/MMLU results show that cost-aware routing can reduce exact-match
accuracy, so correctness-critical workloads need premium pinning or task-type
guardrails.
